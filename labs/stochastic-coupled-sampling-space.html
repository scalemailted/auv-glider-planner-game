1. From oracle coupling to uncertain coupling

The previous coupled-sampling lab used an oracle objective: the true process value, flow, constraints, and mission context were all known. A stochastic coupled sampling space keeps the same field stack, but hides truth behind forecasts, uncertainty, noisy observations, and update cycles.

S*(x,y,t) = h(V_true,F,C,M)
In words: oracle sampling value uses true value, true flow, constraints, and mission goals.
A(x,y,t) = h(μ_post,U,P_unknown,F,C,M)
In words: belief-based acquisition uses what the vehicle currently believes and what it still needs to learn.
Core shift: oracle coupling asks what is truly best. Uncertain coupling asks what is best to do given belief, uncertainty, hidden-event evidence, movement limits, and mission risk.

2. The layer stack: truth, forecast, flow, constraints, belief

The stochastic coupled stack separates true state, expected state, belief state, current flow, and mission constraints.

T(x,y,t)hidden environmental truth
X_true(x,y,t)true latent process state
V_true(x,y,t)true sampling value produced by the process
E(x,y,t)forecast or expected state
μ_prior / μ_postbelief before and after observations
U(x,y,t)uncertainty field used by the belief objective
F(x,y,t) = <u,v>current vector field
C(x,y)land, hazard, depth, or policy constraint
Mmission objective and vehicle limits

3. Oracle objective vs belief objective

The oracle objective is useful for evaluation, benchmarking, and regret. The belief objective is what a fair planner can use during the mission.

Oracle objective

S*(x,y,t) = h(V_true,F,C,M)

Uses the true sampling value. It is the answer key, not the fair planning input.

Belief objective

A(x,y,t) = h(μ_post,U,P_unknown,F,C,M)

Uses posterior belief, expected uncertainty, hidden-event probability, flow, constraints, and mission context.

Oracle vs Belief Objective Widget

Change forecast error and uncertainty, then compare belief choice against the hidden oracle objective.

What to notice: a rational belief-based decision can still have regret after truth is revealed.

4. Expected-state uncertainty and unknown-event probability

This lab uses two different uncertainty maps. They should not be merged conceptually.

U_expected(x,y,t)

Uncertainty about a forecasted or represented state. It asks: how unsure are we about something the model already knows to represent?

P_unknown(x,y,t)

Probability that an unmodeled event or hidden phenomenon exists. It asks: is there evidence for something the model did not know to look for?

Two-Uncertainty-Maps Widget

Compare expected-state uncertainty, unknown-event probability, and their combined acquisition pressure.

What to notice: high uncertainty about a known front is not the same as evidence for a hidden plume.

5. Forecast error vs hidden unknown

Inaccurate expected state / forecast error: “We were wrong about something we thought we knew.”

Hidden unknown state: “We discovered something we did not know to look for.”

Forecast error

A represented feature is shifted, too weak, too strong, or timed incorrectly. The response is to correct the forecast and update confidence.

Hidden event

A phenomenon was not represented at all. The response is to create a hypothesis layer and gather confirmatory evidence.

Forecast Error vs Hidden Event Coupled Widget

Use surprise, spatial coherence, persistence, signature match, and flow consistency to separate forecast correction from hidden-event discovery.

What to notice: a single surprising sample is not enough. Hidden-event confidence needs coherent evidence.

6. Observations, innovation, and surprise

During a mission, the vehicle does not directly see the full truth. It gets sparse noisy measurements and compares them to forecast expectation.

z_i = T(x_i,y_i,t_i) + ε_i
In words: each observation equals truth at the sampled place and time plus sensor noise.
innovation_i = z_i - E(x_i,y_i,t_i)
In words: innovation is what the observation adds beyond the forecast.
surprise_i = abs(innovation_i) / sqrt(U_expected_i^2 + σ_sensor^2)
In words: surprise is high when the measurement is too different to explain by expected uncertainty and sensor noise.
Plain-language rule: high innovation is a clue; high surprise is a stronger clue; coherent high surprise over space and time is evidence.

7. Flow consistency and hidden-event evidence

Hidden-event evidence should respect physics. If an anomaly appears stretched along the current field, persists across surfacing cycles, and matches the sensor signature, it is more credible than isolated noise.

hidden_event_evidence = surprise + spatial_coherence + persistence_over_time + sensor_signature_match + flow_consistency
In words: hidden events become credible when multiple evidence sources agree.
Hidden-event confidence: the mission should treat hidden-event confidence as a derived belief, not as direct truth. It rises when surprise, spatial coherence, persistence, sensor signature, and flow consistency agree.

Flow-Consistent Anomaly Widget

Adjust anomaly alignment, persistence, and sensor signature match.

What to notice: flow consistency can turn surprise into a physical hidden-event hypothesis.

8. Building an acquisition field

An acquisition field is a planning surface. It rewards expected value, learning value, hidden-event detection, and forecast validation while penalizing cost and risk.

A(x,y,t) = h(μ_post,U,P_unknown,F,C,M) where h may combine posterior value, uncertainty reduction, hidden-event probability, flow assistance, constraints, mission priority, travel cost, and safety risk.

Acquisition Composer Widget

Change the objective weights and watch the suggested sampling target move.

What to notice: a planner that only chases high posterior value may ignore high-value learning opportunities.

9. Constraint-aware and reachability-aware value

A cell can be scientifically valuable but unreachable, unsafe, too costly, or impossible to sample before the deadline. Coupled sampling must account for C(x,y), current-assisted movement, fuel, surfacing cadence, depth, land, hazards, and mission time.

A_reachable(x,y,t) = A(x,y,t) * reachable(x,y,t,M,F,C)
In words: executable value is acquisition value filtered by whether the glider can actually get there and sample safely.

Reachable Acquisition Widget

Adjust time budget, flow assist, and cost weight to see how reachable value differs from raw value.

What to notice: the best science cell is not automatically the best mission waypoint.

10. Surfacing, updates, and replanning

Gliders surface periodically. That creates a mission cycle: collect observations, transmit, assimilate, update posterior belief and uncertainty, rebuild acquisition, then replan.

11. Oracle comparison and regret

Regret compares the route chosen under belief with the route that would have been chosen if the oracle truth had been available.

regret = S*(chosen_oracle_best) - S*(chosen_by_belief)
In words: regret is how much true value was lost because the planner had to act under incomplete belief.

Oracle Regret Comparison Widget

Choose a belief route, then reveal oracle truth to see regret.

What to notice: low regret can mean a good forecast, a robust acquisition function, or luck.

12. Example mission situations

Shifted front

Forecast error dominates. Sample across the front to estimate displacement and update μ_post.

Hidden plume

P_unknown(x,y,t) dominates. Follow flow-consistent anomalies and gather confirming samples.

Strong current corridor

High value may be unreachable against flow. Acquisition must respect F(x,y,t) and energy limits.

Risky high value

Hazards or shallow water can make a high posterior value cell a poor mission choice.

13. How this connects to Planner / Mission Evaluation

Planner / Mission Evaluation should compare route choices using value collected, cost, uncertainty reduction, hidden-event discovery, route feasibility, and regret against oracle evaluation.

Planner inputA(x,y,t), reachability, vehicle model, surfacing cadence
Simulation outcomeexecuted path, observations, updates, score
Evaluationoracle value, learning value, regret, constraint violations
Fairness rule: fair planners can use forecasts, posterior belief, uncertainty, and observations. They should not use T(x,y,t) or V_true(x,y,t) unless explicitly labeled oracle.
