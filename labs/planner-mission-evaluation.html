1. From fields to routes

The previous labs produced fields. This lab turns fields into actions. Fields tell the planner where value is, where uncertainty is, where flow helps or hurts, where hazards exist, and where time-sensitive targets appear. A route tells the vehicle where to go, when to go, and what to do there.

A(x,y,t) = acquisition or sampling objective
In words: A is the map of opportunity.
P = {p_0, p_1, ..., p_T}
In words: P is the ordered route through that map.

Field-to-Route Overview Widget

Toggle value, flow, hazards, and route layers to see how a plan sits on top of the field stack.

What to notice: the route is not drawn on an empty map. It responds to layered fields.

2. What is a waypoint plan?

A waypoint is an intended place, time, and action. A plan is an ordered list of waypoints. A plan is not the same thing as the actual path; the actual path may drift, miss targets, or stop early during simulation.

p_t = (x_t, y_t, time_t, action_t)
In words: each waypoint is a planned decision at a place and time.
P = {p_0, p_1, ..., p_T}
In words: a route is a sequence of planned decisions.

navigation

A commanded underwater movement intent.

surface

A communication/update point.

samplingTarget

A science target marker; it must be route-connected to become executable.

terminalCarryThrough

A final command that continues until mission time expires.

Waypoint Sequence Widget

Reorder or delete waypoints and watch ETA/cost change.

What to notice: the same points in a different order can have a different cost and score.

3. Reward, cost, risk, and constraints

Planning is a tradeoff. A useful route collects value, but it also spends time and energy and may pass through risk.

objective(P) = sum_t reward(p_t,t) - lambda_cost cost(P) - lambda_risk risk(P) - penalties(P)
In words: the route score balances what you gain against what the route costs.
Rewardsample value, priority targets, information gain, hidden-event confirmation
Costtravel time, energy, distance, cross-current effort
Riskhazards, shallow water, shoreline risk, strong currents, low confidence
Constraintsland, no-go cells, duration, fuel, deployment, recovery rules

Reward-Cost Tradeoff Widget

Change reward, cost, and risk weights for three candidate targets.

What to notice: highest value is not always the best target.

4. Reachability and timing

A high-value region can be useless if the vehicle cannot reach it in time. Timing matters for moving targets, temporal Gold Stars, active fronts, forecast updates, surfacing windows, mission end time, and stale/revisit value.

reachable value: a cell's scientific value becomes mission value only when the vehicle can reach it safely, on time, and with enough energy.

arrival_time(current, target)
In words: estimate when the glider reaches a target.
A_reachable(x,y,t) = A(x,y,t) if arrival_time <= target_window, else 0
In words: the target only matters if the vehicle can arrive while it is still useful.
feasible(P) = route obeys time, energy, terrain, safety, and mission constraints
In words: a route is feasible only when it can actually be executed under the rules.

Reachability Timing Widget

Adjust time budget, vehicle speed, target window, and flow assist.

What to notice: changing the time budget can change the best target.

5. Flow-aware planning

Currents are not just visual arrows. They affect drift, speed over ground, energy, cross-current risk, ETA, route shape, and surfacing prediction.

actual motion ~= commanded motion + driftGain * F(x,y,t)
In words: the current can push the vehicle away from the intended path.
effective_cost = base_cost - current_assist + cross_current_penalty
In words: a current can help, resist, or push sideways.

Flow-Aware Route Widget

Compare a direct route with a current-aware route under several flow presets.

What to notice: the shortest route is not always the easiest route.

6. Greedy planning as a baseline

A Greedy Planner chooses the next best-looking local option. It is easy to understand and useful as a baseline, but it is not optimal.

next_target = argmax_i value_i / travel_cost_i
In words: pick the next target with the best immediate payoff ratio.

Greedy Planner Widget

Step or run simple greedy strategies: nearest value, value per distance, uncertainty per distance, or hidden-event suspicion per distance.

What to notice: greedy decisions can be good locally but miss better long-term routes.

7. Coverage, boundary, and revisit strategies

Not every mission is "go to the hottest cell." Coverage samples broad space, boundary/front following tracks an edge, revisit planning keeps information fresh, station monitoring visits fixed points on cadence, and anomaly follow-up confirms surprising observations.

coverage(P) = unique_area_sampled(P)
In words: broad spatial coverage can be the main reward.
boundary_score(P) = samples_near_front(P)
In words: front missions reward samples near a moving edge.
freshness_score(P) = reduction_in_age_of_information(P)
In words: revisit missions reward reducing stale information.

Strategy Comparison Widget

Run the same map under greedy value, coverage, boundary, revisit, and uncertainty-reduction strategies.

What to notice: the best route depends on the mission question.

8. Uncertainty-aware sampling

A planner may sample where value is high, where uncertainty is high, where observations would reduce uncertainty, or where a high-value forecast needs validation.

score_belief(P) = sum_t A(p_t,t) - costs - penalties
In words: belief score uses the forecast-visible acquisition objective, not hidden truth.

Uncertainty-Aware Planner Widget

Blend expected value with uncertainty or information gain.

What to notice: a route can be valuable because it teaches the planner, not only because it immediately collects reward.

9. Forecast validation vs hidden-event follow-up

Forecast error response corrects an existing expected map, changes confidence, and replans around the corrected state. Hidden unknown response creates or strengthens a new hypothesis, adds confirmatory sampling objective, follows coherent anomalies, and updates unknown-event probability.

Forecast validation

Sample across a represented feature to learn whether it is shifted, too weak, too strong, or mistimed.

Hidden-event follow-up

Gather coherent observations that support or reject a new hypothesis.

Validation vs Discovery Route Widget

Compare a validation transect with a hidden-event follow-up path.

What to notice: forecast validation and hidden-event follow-up answer different questions.

10. Oracle vs belief planners

Oracle planners see truth or true objective and are useful for upper-bound comparison. Belief planners see forecast, posterior, and acquisition fields and are fairer for normal stochastic missions. Truth-assisted planners use some truth information and are useful for debugging only when labeled.

score_oracle(P) = sum_t S*(p_t,t) - costs - penalties
In words: oracle score evaluates a route against true value.
score_belief(P) = sum_t A(p_t,t) - costs - penalties
In words: belief score evaluates what looked good to the fair planner at planning time.

Oracle vs Belief Planner Widget

Switch planner information labels and compare the route that results.

What to notice: a solver that sees truth should not be compared as a fair player baseline.

11. Regret and missed opportunity

Regret measures missed opportunity under imperfect belief. It helps explain whether a route failed because of bad routing, bad forecast, hidden truth, risk, or unlucky stochastic outcome.

regret = score_oracle(P_oracle_best) - score_oracle(P_belief_chosen)
In words: regret is true value missed by acting on imperfect belief.
regret = oracle value missed by acting on imperfect belief
In words: lower regret means the belief route was close to the answer key.

Regret Breakdown Widget

Choose a route and compare its oracle score to the oracle best route.

What to notice: regret separates achieved score from missed opportunity.

12. Surfacing, updates, and replanning

A surface update can change the planning problem. The vehicle may get a GPS fix, transmit observations, receive new forecasts, update belief, discover route failure, and replace future waypoints.

sample
surface
update belief
validate route
replan
P_future = replan(current_state, μ_post, U, A, F, C, M)
In words: after surfacing, future commands should reflect the updated state and belief.

Surface Replanning Widget

Apply forecast correction, hidden discovery, or route-failure recovery after surfacing.

What to notice: replanning changes future waypoints, not the already-executed past.

13. Multi-agent planning

Multi-agent planning is not just "run the same route three times." A team should split work, avoid duplicate sampling when it is not useful, share mission reward, and account for different starts or vehicle limits.

P_team = {P_1, P_2, ..., P_N}
In words: a team plan is one route per glider.
maximize sum_agents value(P_a) - duplicate_penalty - cost - risk
In words: team value rewards useful work and penalizes duplicated effort.

Multi-Agent Split Widget

Change agent count and route-splitting strategy.

What to notice: more vehicles do not automatically mean better results if they duplicate work.

14. Simulation is the referee

Planning preview estimates route outcome. Simulation executes it. It may reveal current drift, missed waypoints, fuel failure, terrain blocks, hazard contact, stochastic ROI success/failure, forecast mismatch, hidden-event discovery, duplicate penalties, or time expiration.

planned path != actual path
In words: the route is the command; the simulated path is what actually happened.
actual motion ~= commanded motion + driftGain * current + optional stochastic perturbation
In words: simulation applies the environment model to the plan.
Plain language: the preview helps you plan. The simulation is the referee.

Planned vs Actual Path Widget

Change current strength and drift noise to compare planned and actual paths.

What to notice: a route can look valid in planning but perform differently in simulation.

15. Debrief and mission evaluation

Debrief turns execution into learning. It should explain what the plan collected, what it missed, what it cost, what hazards it hit, whether uncertainty caused regret, and whether another planner would have done better.

score = collected_value - energy_cost - hazard_penalty - missed_objective_penalty + bonuses
In words: score evaluates the mission outcome.
regret = oracle_score - achieved_score
In words: regret explains missed opportunity under imperfect knowledge.

forecast regret

Debrief Scorecard Widget

Choose route-result scenarios and compare score components.

What to notice: debrief is not just punishment. It explains why the route behaved the way it did.

17. Example mission strategies

Front tracking

Priority: sample moving boundary/front.

Route: cross-front transects or along-boundary tracking.

Metric: front samples, timing, coverage, energy.

Common mistake: chasing old front position after forecast error.

Bloom confirmation

Priority: sample suspected biological signal.

Route: confirmatory transect, then downstream follow-up.

Metric: hidden-event confidence, coherent anomaly samples, regret.

Common mistake: overreacting to one noisy spike.

Plume source localization

Priority: move up-gradient or up-current after confirmation.

Route: cross-plume transects, source narrowing.

Metric: confirmation, source proximity, cost, hazard exposure.

Common mistake: treating a hidden plume like a known static hotspot.

Coverage survey

Priority: sample broad area.

Route: lawnmower or space-filling coverage.

Metric: coverage, duplicate avoidance, energy.

Common mistake: overfocusing on local hotspots.

Revisit monitoring

Priority: keep information fresh.

Route: periodic station revisit.

Metric: age-of-information reduction, missed revisit windows.

Common mistake: revisiting too soon or too late.

Flow-assisted sampling

Priority: use current assist while avoiding drift risk.

Route: downstream or cross-current-aware sequence.

Metric: energy saved, ETA accuracy, missed waypoint risk.

Common mistake: choosing shortest geometric path instead of lowest effective cost.
